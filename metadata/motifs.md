# Structural arc and motif map

Relay Fault follows a switching network that keeps doing its job while pressure alters the timing of its replies. The pulse is established with a complete ensemble at the outset. Returns are recognisable working states, not introductions rebuilt from silence.

| Motif | Character | Musical identity | Principal channel |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A — Drive shaft | Insistent low torque | Root, root, fifth, octave-implied bass pulses; short gaps before snare accents | Ch2 |
| B — Contact bounce | A deliberate signal followed by an imperfect reply | E–G–B opening and D–B–G answer; finite phrases with rests | Ch3 |
| C — Load indicator | A lamp that changes before the mechanism reacts | Low filter stabs at phrase starts and periodic metallic ticks | Ch4 |
| D — Overcurrent | A near-neighbour under strain | F against the remembered E centre, with quicker bass attacks | Ch2/Ch3 |
| E — Release latch | A last, decisive operation | Contracting drum spaces, descending lead answer, final E impact | All, in the coda |

A and B are melodic single-note lines; they may overlap across channels but never require a chord sample. Percussion shares channels with selected voices only at explicit handoffs. C is finite, not a sustained pad.

The first three-pattern group establishes E, confirms its response and turns through D. A C–D–E group broadens the narrative without a breakdown. The pressure group holds E, leans briefly onto F and returns to E with the densest response. Repeated groups form the working mechanism. The last three-pattern group starts from C, tightens through D and releases onto E with a composed flourish.

The harmonic language is E-centred minor with a Phrygian F inflection, most exposed in the pressure group. The F pattern avoids an F-against-B exposed tritone in its main melodic statement. Pattern boundaries mostly enter on clear roots; no pattern depends on a held note or an inherited slide.
